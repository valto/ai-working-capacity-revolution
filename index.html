Why Are They Spending Trillions on AI?

The numbers and economics behind the AI working-capacity revolution — from electricity and infrastructure to tokens, robots, value, ownership, and agency.

Author: Valto Loikkanen  ·  Licence: CC BY 4.0  ·  Factual source cut-off: 2026-08-13

Model version: v1.0.1 (last tagged release, 2026-08-14)  ·  this site tracks the repository's main branch, which may be ahead of that tag. See CHANGELOG for what has changed since, and the repository's Releases page for citable, checksum-verified snapshots of every version. The conceptual framework (energy → hardware → compute → models → tokens → AI working capacity → work → outcomes → value → agency; Advise → Cowork → Delegate → Lead; Individual → Team → AI workforce) is intended to stay durable across versions — the numerical inputs (hardware price, electricity price, financing cost, utilization, human labor cost, and similar) are expected to keep changing. Any published figure from this package should be read as "under these stated assumptions, at this model version, the calculation gives X" — not as a fixed fact independent of version or scenario.

A neutral-observer research synthesis reverse-engineering a connected economic model — energy → hardware → compute → models → tokens → AI working capacity → digital work → outcomes → value → agency — from public statements by Jensen Huang, Sam Altman, and Mark Zuckerberg, combined with independently verified hardware specs, pricing, and benchmarks. Every substantive claim is tagged with one of five evidence classes (OBSERVED FACTATTRIBUTED STATEMENTDERIVED CALCULATIONSCENARIO ASSUMPTIONINTERPRETATION) so a reader can tell, at a glance, what is independently verified, what is someone's on-record claim, what is arithmetic, and what is an editable illustration.

It is not a vendor report, a political programme, or an investment recommendation. Six reader lenses — Individual, SME/owner-manager, Finance/infrastructure investor, Government/region/community, AI builder/operator, and Educator/researcher/journalist — are given equal weight and no priority ordering, by deliberate editorial decision.
